Open the application and take a few minutes to get oriented. You do not need to learn everything at once.

Start with a new document. You will be asked to choose a size and a color mode. For anything headed to a screen, choose RGB. For anything headed to a printer, choose CMYK. If you are unsure, RGB is the safer starting point for practice work.

The workspace is organized around an artboard — the white area in the middle where your artwork lives. Everything outside the artboard is scratch space where you can park elements you are still deciding on.

On the left side you will find the toolbar. The selection arrow, the pen tool, the shape tools, and the type tool are the four you will reach for most often. On the right side you will find panels for color, layers, and stroke settings.

Draw a rectangle, change its fill color, and add a line of text. That small exercise touches most of the core parts of the application and gives you a feel for how things connect.

## 🎨 Popular Workflows

**Building a logo from scratch.** Sketch the idea on paper first, then rebuild it using simple shapes. Combine shapes with the pathfinder tools, adjust corners, and finish by adding type. Keep the logo in a single color first; color comes later.

**Designing a business card.** Set up a document at 3.5 by 2 inches with a small bleed area around the edges. Place your logo, add the contact details, and export as a print-ready file. Check that all text sits comfortably inside the safe margin.

**Creating a poster.** Work at the final print size rather than scaling up later. Place artwork, set the headline type, and review the composition from a distance by zooming out. Print a small test copy if you can.

**Making social media graphics.** Create a document at the platform's recommended dimensions, keep important elements away from the edges, and export at a size that stays sharp on high-resolution screens.

**Lettering a custom headline.** Type the word, convert it to outlines, then adjust individual anchor points until the letterforms feel right. This is where vector editing shines — every curve stays editable.

## 🛠️ Tips for a Smooth Experience

Save early and save often. Use the Save As option to keep multiple versions of a project so you can return to an earlier idea without losing your current work.

Name your layers. A project with twelve layers called "Layer 1" through "Layer 12" becomes difficult to navigate quickly. A few seconds of renaming saves minutes of hunting.

Use guides and alignment tools rather than eyeballing positions. Consistent spacing is one of the clearest signs of professional work, and the application makes it easy to achieve.

Keep a personal swatch library of your brand colors so every project starts from the same palette.

Zoom out regularly. Small misalignments are hard to spot when you are zoomed in close, but they become obvious at a normal viewing size.

Export a copy in a common format as well as your working file, so you can share previews with people who do not have the application installed.
